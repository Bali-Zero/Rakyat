## The Facts

Landbank reported on 19 November 2026 that investment realised in Bali’s hotel and restaurant sector during January–September 2026 totalled approximately Rp12.49 trillion. It reported Rp7.31 trillion from domestic investment and Rp5.18 trillion from foreign investment, rounded to 59% and 41%. These figures are attributed to that report; the underlying detailed dataset has not been independently reproduced here.

A share of investment flows over nine months is different from a share of existing buildings, operating businesses or land ownership. The figures therefore cannot support the claim that domestic investors own 59% of Bali’s hotels.

---

## In Practice

When assessing a hotel or restaurant proposal, ask which geographic area, period and business classifications a statistic covers. Then compare the project with its actual competitive set: location, room or seat capacity, pricing, operating costs and realistic demand.

Keep capital expenditure separate from operating performance. A larger investment flow does not show occupancy, profit, debt-service capacity or the return an individual investor will receive. For a PT PMA, the proposed business structure and permissions also need their own review.

A useful investment memo puts the cited market figure in one section and the project’s assumptions in another. Mark each assumption—such as nightly rate, utilisation or maintenance cost—so it can be tested instead of being mistaken for reported data.

---

## What This Does Not Establish

These numbers do not prove a guaranteed tourism boom, establish property ownership shares or remove licensing requirements. Reported market commentary and forecasts should remain separate from actual realised investment.
