## The Facts

A Bali developer's account of slower house sales offers a reason to examine a project's evidence carefully. It does not establish that every Bali property segment is falling, or that foreign buyers can obtain a particular type of mortgage.

## What the report actually says

NusaBali's 21 November 2026 report cites BCA Land owner I Gede Suardita speaking on 20 November. He describes more difficult sales and mortgage approval processes, including income documentation challenges for self-employed buyers and requests for larger down payments in some cases.

He also describes holding back on new land purchases while continuing development on land already owned. The report discusses smaller homes as well as other residential stock.

These are attributed observations from a developer, not a comprehensive transaction dataset. They do not prove a market-wide price collapse, a uniform change in bank lending policy, or that Bali holiday villas have the same demand and financing profile.

## Why the distinction matters for foreign investors

A purchase decision needs evidence about the actual asset and buyer. An asking price, an advertised yield and a completed comparable sale answer different questions. So do household mortgages and a company's project financing.

Do not assume that a PT PMA automatically makes a land right, business activity or loan available. Ask an Indonesian legal professional to examine the proposed buyer, property rights, permitted use and contractual structure. Obtain financing eligibility and conditions directly from the lender before relying on them.

## A practical project review

Before paying a reservation fee, request a clear evidence pack:

- Identify the legal seller and the party entitled to grant the proposed rights.
- Compare recent completed transactions for genuinely similar properties, rather than advertised listings alone.
- Separate the price of land rights, construction, furnishings, management and taxes.
- Request the construction timetable, payment milestones and documentation showing progress.
- Ask what happens if completion is delayed, financing fails or a refund is disputed.
- Examine assumptions behind projected occupancy, operating costs and resale proceeds.
- Check who controls payments and which evidence is required before the next instalment.

These are due-diligence questions, not findings that the developer in the report has breached a contract.

## The investor takeaway

Use the report as a prompt to ask better questions. It provides no reliable basis for a guaranteed discount, a prediction of recovery, or advice to buy or sell immediately. A decision should rest on the specific project's documents and independently checked assumptions.
