## The Facts

Indonesia’s workplace safety law is being discussed for revision, with 2027 mentioned as the intended discussion period. For Bali villa, hotel and other business operators, this is a policy development to monitor—not an announcement that a new set of rules has already taken effect.

## What has actually been announced?

In a report published on 23 November 2026, DPR’s official media described Commission IX gathering input on revising Law No. 1 of 1970 on occupational safety. The report concerned a visit to PT ATMI Solo on 20 November and statements by Commission IX deputy chair Nihayatul Wafiroh.

The account referred to plans for discussion in 2027 and highlighted worker protection, including BPJS Ketenagakerjaan coverage. It did not announce an enacted replacement law, a final implementation date or a new compliance deadline for Bali operators.

Do not treat the source publication date, the visit date and the proposed legislative timetable as the same event.

## What can operators review now?

The following is a practical internal review, not a list of newly enacted requirements.

Start with the work actually performed at the property: maintenance, cleaning, electrical work, deliveries and any construction or renovation. Record where workers, contractors and guests might cross paths.

For each task, identify who plans it, who supervises it and who can stop it when conditions are unsafe. Avoid leaving a gap between the property manager’s understanding and the contractor’s understanding.

## Review contractor arrangements

Collect current contractor contact details, scope of work and the person responsible on site. Ask what training, equipment and supervision the assigned task needs.

Schedule disruptive work so that guest access can be managed. Discuss how tools and materials will be stored, how work areas will be separated and how changes to the work plan will be communicated.

These questions are useful even before any proposed law is finalised. They help an operator understand what is happening on the property rather than relying on an informal assurance that “the contractor handles everything.”

## Check records and response plans

Review worker and contractor records with the people responsible for HR, safety and administration. Check the relevant BPJS arrangements and applicable obligations for the actual employment or contracting relationship; this article does not determine eligibility or liability for a particular person.

Make emergency contacts accessible and confirm how an incident would be reported. Keep a record of problems found, corrective actions, responsible people and follow-up dates.

For a foreign director managing a Bali PT PMA remotely, ask for a concise documented review from the local team. A company document folder is more useful when it reflects the current workforce and work site.

## What to watch next

Look for an official bill, its legislative status and any enacted implementing provisions. Until those documents exist, avoid circulating claims about new penalties or compulsory measures supposedly beginning in 2027.

The immediate value of this news is an opportunity to check existing arrangements. The proposed review should be reported accurately as a legislative plan.
