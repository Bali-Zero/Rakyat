## The Facts

## Before a permit application, check whether your records tell the same story

A business can sell products under one brand while its legal documents describe several different activities. For Bali operators involved in production, that is a useful place to start a review of industrial licensing and SIINas records.

On 14 October 2026, Pontianak's municipal government reported a workshop on KBLI 2025 Category C and registration in SIINas, Indonesia's national industrial information system. Officials emphasised matching industrial account information with business legality and checking the applicable requirements. The event took place in Pontianak; it was not the announcement of a new Bali-specific rule. [Official municipal report](https://www.pontianak.go.id/pontianak-hari-ini/berita/Dorong-Pelaku-Industri-Pontianak-Tertib-Administrasi-dan-Perizinan).

The wider reporting framework predates that workshop. Kemenperin's explanation of Permenperin 13/2025 addresses periodic industrial-data reporting through SIINas. Registration and ongoing reporting should therefore be considered separately. [Kemenperin explanation](https://ilmate.kemenperin.go.id/publikasi/informasi-industri/permenperin-132025-diterbitkan-industri-wajib-lapor-data-secara-berkala?type=publication).

## Establish what your company actually does

Bali Zero recommends writing a plain-language production description before asking for a classification check. Identify the products, materials, processing steps, production location and the party performing the work.

For example, a brand may manufacture products itself, purchase finished goods from another producer, or combine several arrangements. Those facts should be made explicit. Selling a product under your label is not enough information to decide which industrial obligations apply to your own entity.

Prepare a simple document map linking the company name, NIB, registered activities, project locations and any SIINas account already used. Flag differences rather than changing records merely to make them look consistent.

## Compare evidence before submitting

Ask the staff member or representative handling the application to show which activity and location the proposed submission covers. Keep the application reference and a copy of the documents actually uploaded.

For a production business, useful review materials may include a process outline, equipment list, product catalogue and details of any contract manufacturer. These are practical preparation suggestions, not a universal statutory attachment list.

If production has moved or the product range has changed, explain that history to the adviser reviewing the file. A current photograph and a short dated description can help distinguish an old administrative entry from the operation now on the ground.

## Keep registration and reporting on separate checklists

Where SIINas obligations apply, ask separately whether the account identity is correct and whether the required reports have been submitted. An account login is not evidence that every reporting obligation has been completed.

Keep submission receipts and any requests for clarification together. Assign a responsible person and a review date so the file does not depend on one employee's inbox or a consultant's verbal confirmation.

Do not assume that every PT PMA, retailer or hospitality operator must follow the same industrial route. The starting point is the company's actual activity and applicable requirements, not foreign ownership alone.

## Check Bali's separate PMA restrictions

Bali's [official provincial release of 24 July 2026](https://www.baliprov.go.id/web/gubernur-koster-batasi-akses-oss-untuk-pma-di-sejumlah-kbli-lindungi-umkm-lokal-dari-persaingan-tidak-sehat) identifies 18 activities closed to new PMA applications through OSS. SIINas registration does not reopen a restricted activity. Check the exact activity and current application status before committing to an investment.

## What this means for Bali investors

Before purchasing equipment, expanding production or commissioning a licensing application, resolve the gaps in the document map. Ask for a written explanation of the activity classification and the remaining steps.

The Pontianak workshop is a timely reminder to check existing records. It does not establish that all manufacturing is high risk, that every permit now requires a new SIINas registration, or that registering guarantees financing or approval.
