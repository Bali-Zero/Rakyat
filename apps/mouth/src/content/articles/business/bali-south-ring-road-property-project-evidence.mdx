## The Facts

## A confirmed milestone needs a date
Badung's public works department recorded a groundbreaking for Jalan Lingkar Selatan, or the South Ring Road, alongside other road works on 27 July 2026. Its [official announcement](https://dpupr.badungkab.go.id/berita/73439-pada-hari-senin-27-juli-2026-bupati-badung-i-wayan-adi-arnawa-s-h-bersama-wakil-bupati-badung-bagus-alit-sucipta-s-h-beserta-jajaran-melaksanakan-ground-breaking-pembangunan-jalan-lingkar-selatan-jls-serta-shortcut-berawa-umalas-kedampang-teuku-umar-barat) is evidence of that milestone.
A groundbreaking is not the same as an operational road. The announcement does not, on its own, establish today's construction percentage, an assured opening date or a future sale price for a nearby villa. This article revisits the project as a property due-diligence topic, rather than presenting the July event as new November news.
## Turn the location pitch into questions
If a sales presentation relies on future road access, ask which official route document supports the claim and how the specific parcel relates to it. A broad label such as South Bali does not show that a property will have direct access to a particular road.
Request a dated explanation of the assumed route, access point and delivery stage. Keep the seller's assumptions separate from confirmed project documents, and check whether any later official information has changed the picture.
## Test the investment without the promised uplift
As an editorial planning exercise, assess a property using its current access and operating conditions first. Keep any potential benefit from future infrastructure in a separate scenario. This avoids treating an uncertain improvement as income already earned.
For example, compare a base case that assumes today's journey times with a conditional case that assumes improved access. Label the second case clearly and avoid assigning a guaranteed price increase simply because the road has been announced.
## The parcel still needs its own review
Project publicity does not answer questions about a property's ownership documents, permitted use, existing access arrangements or building documentation. Have those matters reviewed for the particular parcel and proposed activity before relying on a marketing claim.
Do not infer that every property near the project must be acquired, demolished or compensated in the same way. Those conclusions require specific legal and project evidence.
The useful takeaway is modest: infrastructure can be relevant to an investment decision, but the connection must be demonstrated. Keep the project milestone, the property's actual circumstances and the seller's forecast as three separate parts of the file.
