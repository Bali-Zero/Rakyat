## The Facts

## More occupied nights, softer prices
An October market update attributed to Brixfox puts average Bali Airbnb occupancy at 52.5%, compared with 51.0% in September. Average nightly rates fell from US$259 to US$252, according to [The Bali Times on 6 November](https://thebalitimes.com/the-island/bali-villa-occupancy-rises-in-october-as-some-sellers-cut-prices/).
That is an increase of 1.5 percentage points in reported occupancy alongside a US$7 reduction in the rate measure. The combination does not establish that every owner earned more.
## One island, different rental markets
The same newspaper report puts Uluwatu at 69.8% occupancy and Ubud at 48.2%. These are figures for the areas tracked, not a forecast for a specific villa. The newspaper also reports asking-price reductions among some sales listings. An advertised reduction is not evidence of a completed sale at that price.
The October numbers are secondary reporting of a private dataset. They are not an official census of all Bali accommodation, long-term rentals or owner income. The underlying full October dataset was not independently reproduced for this article.
## Read the method before the return
[Brixfox's own methodology description](https://www.brixfox.com/) says it measures rental activity using Airbnb calendars and distinguishes tracked listings, calendar observations and selected investment cohorts. Its live figures use defined samples and periods. Do not substitute a current website metric for the October figures in the newspaper; their time windows and populations may differ.
Calendar-based market estimates are also different from an operator's bank receipts and accounts. A useful investment review should explain the dataset, geography, observation period and treatment of unavailable nights before comparing properties.
## A practical owner checklist
For your own property, reconcile paid bookings, cancellations, owner-use dates and net receipts. Then account for platform commissions, management fees, maintenance, utilities and other operating costs. This is a practical financial review, not a new reporting requirement.
When reviewing a sales presentation, request the assumptions behind its revenue projection. Ask whether the occupancy and nightly rate describe the same property type, area and season, and whether the quoted return is gross or net of costs.
The October update offers a market signal: more bookings and weaker pricing can occur together. Use it to ask better questions, not to promise a return or extrapolate one month's performance across an entire year.
