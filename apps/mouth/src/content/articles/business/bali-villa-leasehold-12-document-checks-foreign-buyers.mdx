## The Facts

A villa viewing shows the building. A lease review should show what you are actually entitled to use, for how long, and on what terms.

A commercial property newsletter published on 18 November 2026 brought leasehold due diligence back into discussion. This is an educational checklist prompted by that discussion, not news of a newly enacted property rule. The newsletter is not the legal authority for the checks below.

## Start with the type of right

Indonesia’s Basic Agrarian Law distinguishes Hak Milik from rights to lease land for buildings. Article 21 restricts individual Hak Milik ownership to Indonesian citizens; Articles 44–45 address leases for buildings and eligible holders. A foreign buyer should not interpret the marketing word “ownership” as proof of a right to hold Hak Milik.

The exact legal structure and intended use need a document-specific review. A long lease, an extension promise and a right to operate accommodation are not interchangeable.

## Twelve questions before committing

**1. Who is granting the right?** Match the contracting party’s identity to the underlying documents. Ask an independent adviser to establish their authority to grant the proposed lease.

**2. Which land is included?** Reconcile the land record, plot identification, boundaries, access and the area described in the agreement. Do not rely only on a marketing map.

**3. What affects the land?** Ask about mortgages, disputes, existing leases and other interests. Request independent verification through the appropriate records and professional process.

**4. Are all necessary parties involved?** Have the adviser identify any required company authorisations, co-owner approvals or other consents for this specific transaction. Do not assume the same consent checklist fits every owner.

**5. Does the intended use fit?** Describe whether the property will be a private residence, long-term rental or tourist accommodation. Obtain advice on the location, permitted use and applicable operating approvals.

**6. What building documents apply?** Request the relevant building approval and fitness-for-use documentation. Have any older IMB documentation and subsequent changes assessed in context rather than declaring every older building automatically unlawful.

**7. When does the term start and finish?** Check exact dates, handover conditions and whether construction delays consume part of the usable lease period.

**8. How does an extension work?** Identify the decision deadline, price or pricing mechanism, responsible parties and conditions. A reference to a future extension should be read with its actual contractual terms.

**9. Can you transfer or sublet?** Check assignment, subletting, guest use and management restrictions. Match these clauses to the business model you actually intend.

**10. What are you paying for?** Separate the lease price, building works, furniture, deposits and continuing charges. Record who bears each cost and when it becomes payable.

**11. What happens if delivery goes wrong?** Review milestones, defects, delay provisions, termination, refunds and dispute arrangements before committing funds.

**12. What evidence will you keep?** Retain signed versions, verified attachments, payment records and written answers to unresolved questions. Make sure translated explanations match the agreement being signed.

## A practical next step

Create one document folder and one open-questions list for the property. Ask an independent Indonesian legal adviser and relevant technical professionals to resolve the gaps before payment.

For an expat or a PT PMA, the aim is to align the contracting party, property rights, building documents and intended activity. This checklist does not determine a particular property’s legality or replace that review.
