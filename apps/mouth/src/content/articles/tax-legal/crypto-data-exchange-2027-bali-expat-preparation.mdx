## The Facts

## Why your 2026 records matter now

If you live in Bali and use crypto platforms in several countries, preparing for international data exchange starts with a consistent record of your accounts. Waiting until the exchange begins can make missing histories harder to reconstruct.

Indonesia's PMK 108/2025 provides the technical framework for access to financial information, including the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, or CARF. Its preamble identifies 2027 as the first exchange year for 2026 data. Article 18(6) links the first reporting year to when a provider meets the relevant criteria; this is not a blanket statement that every platform everywhere reports simultaneously.

CARF distinguishes reporting crypto-asset service providers from their users. Article 22 covers reportable individuals and entities, identity and tax-residence information, and relevant transactions. The regulation does not make foreign citizenship, living in Bali or owning crypto alone a complete test of reportability. [Read PMK 108/2025 on the DJP website](https://www.pajak.go.id/en/node/119091).

ANTARA reported on 15 October that Indonesia was preparing to begin the exchange in 2027. That is a future implementation milestone, not evidence that international exchanges have already started. [ANTARA report](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5789569/ri-mulai-pertukaran-informasi-pajak-kripto-dimulai-2027).

## Start with an account inventory

Bali Zero recommends listing each platform, the legal name on the account, the country information recorded there and the person responsible for maintaining it. Include accounts you stopped using during 2026, not just the ones still active.

Download available transaction histories and statements. Keep the original exports alongside your working spreadsheet so an adviser can trace a total back to its source. Note missing months, changed account details and any platform migration.

For transfers between your own wallets, retain the evidence connecting both sides. In your working records, distinguish transfers from purchases, sales and rewards. These are bookkeeping labels for review; they do not decide the tax treatment.

## Review residence information before changing declarations

Compare the information held by your providers with the documents you use for tax reporting. If addresses or tax identification numbers differ, record the discrepancy and ask a qualified adviser how to correct it.

Do not select a tax-residence declaration simply because it matches your passport or visa label. Where more than one jurisdiction may be relevant, bring your travel history and existing tax filings to the review. The adviser should assess the applicable rules and your circumstances before recommending changes.

An information-reporting category is not, by itself, a calculation of tax due. Avoid treating every transfer, trade or reward as having identical tax consequences.

## Keep company and personal records separate

For a PT PMA owner, prepare separate inventories for personal accounts and any accounts held by the company. Record the owner of each asset and the supporting transaction documents. Where ownership is unclear, resolve that question before combining balances in a return.

The practical objective is a file that explains the records without guesswork: account inventory, original exports, reconciliations and a short list of unresolved questions. This article does not assert a verified network of 55 active exchange partners or promise that any named foreign platform will transmit a particular user's data.
