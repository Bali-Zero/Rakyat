## The Facts

Indonesia's Sistem Pemungutan Pajak atas Transaksi Digital Luar Negeri (SPP-TDLN) began implementation on 25 September 2026, according to DJP's 1 October official release. November discussion of broader collection should not be described as the system's first launch.

SPP-TDLN collects existing Indonesian VAT on qualifying digital goods and services supplied from overseas to consumers in Indonesia. DJP says the mechanism does not create a new tax or VAT rate. A transfer or remittance does not become taxable merely because a bank processes it through payment infrastructure.

The initial appointed issuers were BRI, Bank Mandiri, BNI, BTN, Bank Syariah Indonesia and LinkAja. That initial list does not establish that every Indonesian bank, wallet or marketplace is an appointed collector. Further appointments depend on the prescribed preparation and appointment process.

DJP's official notice also says transactions already charged VAT by an appointed PMSE collector continue under that arrangement and are outside SPP-TDLN collection for the same transaction. Transactions outside VAT scope or covered by an exemption should not be collected under this mechanism.

---

## How the Payment Chain Works

PMK 49/2026 provides for an issuer to submit information and obtain confirmation through SPP-TDLN. Collection depends on identifying an eligible taxable transaction. The issuer collects the VAT and passes it through the system operator for payment to the state treasury.

DDTCNews's 9 November explanation identifies PT Jalin Pembayaran Nusantara as the operator and describes the sequence from consumer payment to transaction identification, collection and remittance. This is an administrative collection chain, rather than evidence that every payment is charged.

For overseas suppliers, DJP explains that VAT is included in the customer's payable amount and may be collected through a designated intermediary before settlement. The resulting difference in the merchant's settlement is VAT collection, rather than automatically a separate bank fee. The mechanism alone does not automatically create an additional registration obligation for every foreign supplier.

---

## In Practice

Under Article 10 of PMK 49/2026, the collecting party must issue a collection document containing prescribed information. A qualifying document has a status equivalent to a tax invoice. Input-VAT credit remains conditional: relevant contact information must be registered with DJP and the ordinary input-credit requirements must be met.

A bank statement by itself should not be assumed to guarantee a credit. Finance teams need to check the actual document, taxpayer details and transaction treatment. The regulation also provides a route for requesting repayment of VAT that should not have been collected, through the collecting party to the operator.

## Bali Zero Take

### Our Analysis

For Bali residents and businesses, the useful checks are the supplier's VAT treatment, the payment provider's appointment and the evidence attached to a particular charge. A supplier invoice already showing Indonesian VAT is a reason to check an apparent second collection.

The phrase existing tax does not guarantee an unchanged invoice total for every customer. More effective collection may make previously uncollected VAT visible. It does establish that this system should not be presented as a new levy on all transfers.
