## The Facts

A Kontan report dated 14 November 2026 quoted DJP as saying that five banks and one fintech had been integrated into SPP-TDLN. The report described an ongoing stabilization process involving payment-institution data, the collection platform and Coretax.

That figure is a dated implementation snapshot. It should not be presented as a permanent limit, or as evidence that every transaction through those institutions is subject to VAT.

DJP's official guidance describes SPP-TDLN as a mechanism for collecting VAT on qualifying cross-border digital goods and services. It is not a new tax or a new VAT rate.

---

## In Practice

For a Bali company, the practical question is how its provider handles a qualifying payment and supplies the supporting records. Ask the bank or payment provider what information will appear on the transaction statement and where collection evidence can be obtained.

Keep supplier details and payment references aligned. When a payment is made by an employee and reimbursed by the company, preserve the original invoice and transaction trail instead of relying only on a reimbursement spreadsheet.

## Bali Zero Take

### Our Analysis

An integration announcement measures participation in a system; it does not measure the tax treatment of every customer. Nor does a stabilization phase establish that a particular business has an error in Coretax.

Review actual records before diagnosing a mismatch. If one exists, identify whether it concerns timing, currency, supplier information or tax collection, and ask the responsible party to explain it.
