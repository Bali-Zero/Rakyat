## TL;DR

---

## The Facts

PMK 141/PMK.03/2015 lists architectural services among the relevant services. Its Article 1 provides a 2% withholding basis on gross remuneration excluding VAT for services falling within the provision. It also excludes services already subject to final income tax under separate rules and distinguishes services covered by Article 21.

This is an explanation of existing provisions, not an announcement of a new architect tax. A description on an invoice is not enough to conclude that every design, building or construction-related payment receives identical treatment.

---

## Start with three questions

Who is the contracting supplier: an individual, a domestic company or another form of provider? What is the actual scope of work? Is there a separate final-tax treatment relevant to that scope?

Our recommendation is to give the contract, quotation and draft invoice to the company's tax adviser together. This helps avoid making a withholding decision using only the word architect or an informal message about the price.

---

## Build a clear payment file

Keep the supplier's identity and tax information alongside the agreed scope, payment schedule and invoice. Ask the adviser to explain the withholding basis and any exception they apply. Where withholding is required, agree the documentation and payment reconciliation before processing the transfer.

For example, a quotation for design services and a contract covering a wider construction package may raise different classification questions. This article does not assign a tax outcome to a hypothetical package without its documents.

---

## Why this matters for a PT PMA

A property budget is easier to manage when the commercial price, VAT and income-tax withholding are understood separately. Review the particular transaction before agreeing that a quoted total is the amount the supplier will receive. The correct treatment depends on the parties and the service, not simply on the fact that the project is a villa in Bali.
