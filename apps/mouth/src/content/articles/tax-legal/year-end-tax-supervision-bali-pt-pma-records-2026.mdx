## The Facts

DDTC reported on 19 November 2026 that the Directorate General of Taxes was strengthening supervision to support year-end revenue collection. The report describes an administrative focus; it does not announce an audit notice for every business or a separate new tax on foreign-owned companies.

For a business owner, the useful distinction is between a general policy report and a communication addressed to the company. The latter should be checked against its actual reference, sender, requested information and response date.

---

## In Practice

Create a clear trail from transactions to the books and then to submitted returns. Match invoices, bank receipts, expenses, withholding documents and payment references by period. Record the reason for each difference instead of simply making totals look equal.

Ask the finance team to explain unusual year-end movements: deposits, cancellations, related-party balances, large asset purchases or payments spanning more than one accounting period. These are suggested reconciliation questions, not a claim that any one transaction triggers an audit.

If an official request arrives, preserve the original message and have the responsible tax professional confirm its scope and response process through official channels. Keep copies of the documents and explanations actually submitted. A director should be able to understand the explanation without relying solely on a spreadsheet total.

---

## What This Does Not Establish

A revenue-collection announcement does not establish that a particular PT PMA has underpaid tax, will be audited, or owes a penalty. Any correction should follow the company’s facts and the applicable procedure.
