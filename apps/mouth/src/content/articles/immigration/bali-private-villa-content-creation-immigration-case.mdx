## The Facts

A private villa is a setting, not an exemption from the rules that apply to an activity. A reported Uluwatu livestreaming case offers a reason for foreign residents to separate accommodation, immigration permission and the nature of the content they produce.

## What has been reported

BaliNews reported on 24 November 2026 that a French national identified as EOM was deported on 18 November after immigration authorities investigated adult livestreaming from a villa in Uluwatu.

The report attributes the account to the Bali regional immigration office and its head, Ramdhani. It says authorities considered the activity a misuse of a D12 visit stay permit. It also reports a proposed entry ban; a proposal should not be described as a confirmed ban already entered into force.

The original official release and any court record were not obtained for this article. The account is therefore presented as reported immigration action, not an independent finding of criminal guilt.

## Three questions that should stay separate

**Where is the activity happening?** Renting or owning accommodation explains access to the premises. It does not by itself determine whether every activity there is permitted.

**What does the person actually do?** Describe the real activity, including its purpose, participants, commercial arrangements and distribution. Labels such as “private,” “online” or “personal account” do not answer every relevant question.

**What permission covers it?** Check the applicable immigration category and any other requirements against those facts. A company interest, rental contract or audience based overseas should not be treated as a substitute for that assessment.

## Avoid conclusions the case does not support

The reporting does not establish a general ban on foreign content creators, remote work or filming inside rented accommodation. Nor should it be converted into a claim that every unpaid post is unlawful.

At the same time, an absence of payment is not a reliable shortcut for deciding all questions about content or permitted activity. Get advice on the specific facts rather than relying on a social-media summary.

## A practical check before a production

Prepare a short description of what will be filmed, who participates, where it will be distributed and how it is funded. Review the actual permission documents. Clarify venue consent and any production conditions in writing.

If the proposed activity is materially different from the purpose previously explained in an application, seek clarification before proceeding. Keep personal information and consent records secure.

For property operators, a guest's booking is not a legal assessment of the guest's production. Ask clear questions about planned use without assuming misconduct based on nationality or occupation.
