## The Facts

Detik Bali reported on 17 November that an Armenian man identified as DK had been deported on 16 November after immigration authorities found activities connected with work at a beauty clinic in Canggu. The report described him as holding a B1 visitor visa with validity stated through 26 November.

This was reported as a case involving activities inconsistent with the stay permission. It should not be retold simply as an overstay case, and the public report does not provide every detail of the person's immigration file.

---

## A practical distinction for expats

Official immigration information identifies B1 as a tourism-visit category. When considering an activity, check the actual category and its stated permissions rather than relying only on an unexpired date.

A clinic's permission to operate and an individual foreigner's permission to perform an activity are separate questions to examine. One document should not be assumed to answer both.

---

## Before accepting a role

Our recommendation is to describe the proposed tasks in plain language: who receives the service, where it is performed and which organization is involved. Take that description and your actual immigration documents to an appropriately qualified adviser.

Avoid relying on informal labels such as helping out if those words do not accurately describe the work. Ask for a clear assessment of the intended activity before beginning it.

---

## For clinic and hospitality operators

Include immigration-document review in planning for any foreign person's proposed role. Keep the factual job description available so the review addresses actual duties rather than a convenient title.

This case is not evidence that every foreign visitor to a clinic is working, or that every clinic employing foreigners is unlawful. The lesson is to examine the person's activities and permission together, without generalizing beyond the reported facts.
