## The Facts

Indonesia's immigration framework offers two primary residence permit tracks for foreign retirees: the Retirement KITAS, a temporary stay permit (renewed annually for the E33F, granted for five years for the E33E), and the Retirement KITAP, a permanent stay permit that provides multi-year or indefinite residency once eligibility thresholds are met. The KITAP — Kartu Izin Tinggal Tetap — represents the upper tier of Indonesia's legal residence hierarchy for foreigners.

To qualify for a Retirement KITAP, an applicant must generally have held a valid Retirement KITAS for a minimum of three consecutive years, together with an Integration Statement, without significant interruption. This tenure requirement is a hard prerequisite: immigration authorities expect applicants to demonstrate genuine long-term commitment to residing in Indonesia rather than using the country as a periodic base.

Financial thresholds form the second pillar of eligibility. Retirement KITAS holders must demonstrate income or a pension of at least USD 3,000 per month, as the regulation states, plus a bank statement for the last 3 months showing at least USD 2,000. A clean criminal record and valid health insurance are also standard requirements; the E33E additionally requires a USD 50,000 deposit in the applicant's own name at a state-owned (BUMN) Indonesian bank, kept for as long as the permit is valid, while the E33F has no deposit; the E33F also needs a local sponsor, while the E33E is self-filed under an immigration guarantee.

The KITAP, once granted, is typically valid for five years and renewable, with some holders ultimately eligible for an indefinite stay permit. Crucially, KITAP holders are permitted to re-enter Indonesia freely without needing to apply for a re-entry permit each time — a practical advantage that regular KITAS holders do not enjoy.

However, the Retirement KITAP does not confer the right to work. Indonesia maintains a strict separation between its retirement and employment visa categories. Retirees found to be engaging in paid or unpaid professional activities risk permit revocation and potential deportation. The permit is designed exclusively for individuals who live on income or a pension of at least USD 3,000 per month from outside Indonesia's domestic labor market.
