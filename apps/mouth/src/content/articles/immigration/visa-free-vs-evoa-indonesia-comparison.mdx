# Visa-Free Entry vs E-VOA Indonesia 2026: Complete Comparison Guide

## Introduction: The Critical Choice That Affects Your Entire Trip

When you land at Ngurah Rai International Airport in Bali or Soekarno-Hatta in Jakarta, you face a decision that shapes the flexibility and success of your Indonesian trip. Two routes are open to many short-stay visitors: **Visa-Free Entry** and **E-VOA (Electronic Visa on Arrival)**, the electronic route to the B1 Visa on Arrival. Choose wrong, and you could find yourself forced to leave the country after 30 days with no extension available.

This is not a trivial choice. Each route has its own rules and consequences. The confusion is real: many travellers eligible for B1 choose visa-free entry to "save money", only to discover they are stuck when they need five more days. Others apply for B1 when visa-free entry would have been enough.

**This guide removes that confusion.** We compare both options, give you a decision framework, flag common pitfalls and show you how to choose based on your nationality, trip length and need for flexibility. The key numbers are short: both routes start with **30 days**; visa-free entry cannot be extended, while B1 can be extended **once, by up to 30 days, to a maximum of 60 days**.

## Visa-Free Entry: Deep Dive

### What Is Visa-Free Entry?

Visa-free entry (also called "visa exemption") allows citizens of select countries to enter Indonesia without a visa for **tourism purposes only**. It is automatic: no application, no visa fee, no paperwork. Immigration stamps your passport, and you have 30 days from entry.

### Key Features

**Duration:**

- **30 days** from date of entry
- **Non-extendable** under any circumstances
- **Single entry** (leaving and re-entering ends that admission)

**Cost:**

- **IDR 0** (no visa fee)

**Eligibility:**

- **ASEAN countries:** Brunei, Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam
- **Other countries:** Chile, Ecuador, Hong Kong SAR, Macau SAR, Morocco, Peru, Vietnam (some overlap)
- **Total:** Approximately 20 countries (exact list updated periodically by Indonesian Ministry of Law and Human Rights)

**Permitted Activities:**

- Tourism
- Social/cultural visits
- Family visits
- Transit
- **NOT permitted:** Business meetings, work, paid activities, long-term study

**Restrictions:**

- Cannot be extended (ever)
- Cannot be converted to another visa type while in Indonesia
- Cannot work or conduct business
- Overstaying results in daily fines and possible deportation

### Who Should Use Visa-Free Entry?

**Ideal for:**

- Weekend getaways (3-7 days)
- Vacation trips of up to 30 days
- Transit passengers
- Travellers with flexible exit dates who can leave by day 30
- Budget-conscious tourists from eligible countries

**NOT suitable for:**

- Trips where you might want to stay 31+ days
- Digital nomads working remotely (not allowed)
- Travellers with uncertain return flights
- Anyone who values flexibility over avoiding the B1 application

### The 30-Day Wall: Why It Matters

Once you enter on visa-free, you hit a fixed wall at day 30. There is **no extension process**, **no emergency procedure**, **no "just five more days"** option. Your choices are:

1. **Exit by day 30** (comply with rules)
2. **Overstay and face daily fines** and deportation risk
3. **Leave and re-enter**, which is a new entry and not a guaranteed outcome: immigration decides at the border

Many travellers learn this the hard way when they meet someone special, find a surf spot they love, or simply underestimate how addictive Bali becomes.

## E-VOA: Deep Dive

### What Is E-VOA?

E-VOA is the electronic application route for the B1 Visa on Arrival. It is not a separate 60-day visa. B1 grants **30 days** from arrival and can be extended **once, by up to 30 more days**, for a **maximum of 60 days**. It is single-entry: leaving Indonesia ends that admission. Eligible travellers can also obtain a VOA at designated arrival checkpoints.

### Key Features

**Duration:**

- **30 days** initial stay
- **One extension:** up to 30 more days (maximum 60 days from arrival)
- There is **no second B1 extension**
- The extension is a request to immigration, not an entitlement, and involves an immigration-office visit

**Cost:**

- **Government fee:** check the current bill on the official portal ([evisa.imigrasi.go.id](https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id))
- **Bali Zero catalogue prices:** IDR 750,000 for the B1 Visa on Arrival and IDR 850,000 for the +30-day extension, with the government fee included in each

**Eligibility:**

- **90+ countries** including USA, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, EU countries, South Korea, Japan, China, India, Russia, Brazil, South Africa, UAE, and many more
- Check the current official list on the Indonesian immigration website (imigrasi.go.id)

**Application Methods:**

1. **Online pre-arrival:** via the official e-VOA portal (recommended)
2. **On arrival:** at the VOA counter of an eligible checkpoint (may have queues)

**Permitted Activities:**

- Tourism
- **NOT permitted:** paid employment, study, journalism

**Restrictions:**

- B1 is not a work authorisation, including remote work
- It cannot be extended more than once
- Extensions require an immigration-office visit

### Extension Process

**The one extension (days 31-60):**

- File **before day 30**. Ngurah Rai publishes filing no later than seven days before expiry; reconfirm the deadline with the office handling your application
- Start through the official stay-permit claim page, update your accommodation address, submit the requested documents and pay the official bill
- Expect an immigration-office visit for a photo and interview
- Result: if approved, your stay is extended by up to 30 days, to a maximum of 60 days from arrival

**There is no second extension.** After day 60 you must leave, or hold a different visa suited to your activity.

**Critical notes:**

- The extension is **not automatic**: you must apply, and approval remains with immigration
- Missing the deadline results in overstay fines
- An agent can prepare documents and communicate with the office; an agent cannot guarantee approval or remove your attendance obligation

### Who Should Use E-VOA?

**Ideal for:**

- Eligible visits that may run past 30 days, up to 60 days
- Travellers with uncertain plans
- Anyone wanting the option of one extension

**NOT suitable for:**

- Stays planned beyond 60 days
- Work of any kind, including remote work
- Repeated back-to-back B1 entries as a way to stay long term

**The flexibility premium:** B1 costs you the application up front for the **option** to stay longer. Even if you only stay 25 days, you have not lost the right to leave early.

## Decision Matrix: Which to Choose?

Use this decision tree to choose correctly:

### Step 1: Check Your Nationality

**Question:** Is your country on the visa-free list?

- **No** → You don't have a choice. Use B1 / E-VOA (if eligible) or check which visa suits you.
- **Yes** → Continue to Step 2.

### Step 2: Determine Your Trip Length

**Question:** How many days will you stay in Indonesia?

- **1-30 days AND you are 100% certain** → Visa-free (no application)
- **1-30 days BUT uncertain** → B1 / E-VOA (keeps the one extension available)
- **31-60 days** → B1 / E-VOA with the one extension (visa-free cannot cover this)
- **61+ days** → Neither is enough. Assess a different visa before you travel.

### Step 3: Assess Flexibility Needs

**Question:** How much do you value flexibility?

- **High flexibility (plans may change)** → B1 / E-VOA
- **Low flexibility (rigid schedule)** → Visa-free is acceptable (if 30 days or less)

### Step 4: Consider the Cost

**Question:** Does the B1 fee matter for your budget?

- **Yes** → Visa-free if you are certain to stay 30 days or less
- **No** → B1 / E-VOA for the option of an extension. Check the current government fee on the official portal.

## Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

### Mistake 1: "I'll just get visa-free to avoid the application"

**Scenario:** John from Malaysia can enter visa-free or on a B1, and chooses visa-free. On day 20, he falls in love with Uluwatu and wants to stay two more weeks (44 days total).

**Problem:** Visa-free cannot be extended or converted. John must:

- Exit Indonesia by day 30
- Or overstay and face daily fines and deportation risk
- Or leave and re-enter, where immigration decides at the border

**Solution:** If there is **any chance** you will want to stay past day 30, apply for B1 from day 1, so that the one extension is available.

### Mistake 2: "I'll extend visa-free at the immigration office"

**Scenario:** Lisa from Singapore enters visa-free for a "quick 20-day trip." She decides to stay 40 days and visits immigration on day 25 to extend.

**Problem:** Immigration says, "Visa-free cannot be extended. Exit by day 30 or face fines."

**Reality:** There is **no extension process** for visa-free. Immigration offices cannot help. Agents cannot help.

### Mistake 3: "B1 gives me two months, so I will plan 60 days"

**Scenario:** David arrives on B1 and plans 60 days.

**Reality:** B1 is **30 days** on entry. The remaining 30 days exist only if the one extension is requested and approved. David must file on time and keep his departure flexible.

### Mistake 4: Waiting until the last minute to extend B1

**Scenario:** Emma has B1 and waits until day 28 to start the extension. The immigration office is closed for a holiday and she overstays.

**Solution:** File well before your recorded expiry. Ngurah Rai publishes filing no later than seven days before expiry. Holidays, weekends and unexpected closures happen.

### Mistake 5: Not checking nationality eligibility

**Scenario:** Ahmed from Pakistan arrives expecting visa-free or B1. Immigration denies entry because Pakistan is not eligible for either.

**Reality:** ~150 countries can use visa-free or B1, but **not all**. Some nationalities require pre-approved visas.

**Solution:** Check imigrasi.go.id **before** booking flights.

## Airport Process Differences

### Visa-Free Entry Process

**Step-by-step:**

1. **On the plane:**
   - Fill out customs declaration card (provided by airline)
   - No visa paperwork needed

2. **At immigration hall:**
   - Look for the "Visa Free / Visa Exemption" lane (usually separate from the B1 lane)
   - Join the queue

3. **At immigration counter:**
   - Hand over passport (must have 6+ months validity)
   - Officer may ask:
     - "How long will you stay?" (say 30 days or less)
     - "Where are you staying?" (hotel name)
     - "Do you have a return ticket?" (yes, have proof if asked)
   - Officer stamps passport with visa-free entry stamp
   - **Duration:** 30 days from entry date

4. **Baggage claim and exit:**
   - Collect luggage
   - Proceed through customs (green lane if nothing to declare)
   - Exit airport

### B1 / E-VOA Process

**Step-by-step:**

1. **Before flight (recommended):**
   - Open the official portal: [evisa.imigrasi.go.id](https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id)
   - Fill in the application: passport details, travel info, photo
   - Pay the current official bill only through the portal's payment flow
   - Save the issued e-VOA, with a digital backup and a printed copy

2. **On the plane:**
   - Fill out customs declaration card
   - Keep your e-VOA accessible (phone or printout)

3. **At immigration hall:**
   - **If you applied online:** follow the signs for e-VOA holders
   - **If you did not:** check whether your nationality and checkpoint allow VOA, and follow the airport's current instructions

4. **At immigration counter:**
   - Show passport + e-VOA
   - Officer stamps passport
   - **Duration:** 30 days from entry date. Check the stay-permit expiry recorded on entry: it is the date that counts, not the visa's entry-validity window

5. **Baggage claim and exit:**
   - Same as visa-free process

**Pro tip:** Apply online before you fly rather than relying on a counter, and do not assume a payment receipt is an issued visa.

## What If Neither Is Enough?

### Scenario 1: You Need More Than 60 Days

**Options:**

1. **C1 tourist visit visa:**
   - A separate single-entry visa, initially up to 60 days, extendable under its own rules up to 180 days in total
   - It is a different visa, not a longer B1: check its application requirements before you travel
   - [C1 rules](https://jakartapusat.imigrasi.go.id/layanan/warga-negara-asing-wna/visa-republik-indonesia/c1-visa-wisata)

2. **Repeated B1 entries:**
   - **Not recommended.** B1 is single-entry, and each new entry is a fresh decision by immigration at the border
   - Planning a long stay as a chain of 60-day B1 visits invites questions and is not a guaranteed route

3. **KITAS (Limited Stay Permit):**
   - For work, investment, retirement, or family
   - 1-2 years, extendable
   - Requires sponsorship (employer, company, spouse)
   - **Recommended for long-term residents**

### Scenario 2: You Want to Work Remotely

B1 and visa-free entry are not work authorisations, and that includes remote work. Do not plan to work on either. Assess a visa suited to the actual activity **before** you start working.

**Options to assess:**

1. **KITAS Investor:**
   - Start a PT PMA (foreign-owned company) in Indonesia
   - Sponsor yourself for KITAS
   - Legally allowed to work for your company

2. **KITAS Retirement (55+ years old):**
   - Age 55+, proof of income/savings
   - Can live in Indonesia long-term (1 year, renewable)
   - Cannot work

**Reality check:** Tourist routes are for tourism. Treating them as a base for remote work is not a safe plan.

- **Duration:** 30 days, plus one extension of up to 30 days if approved (60 days maximum)
- **Process:** Apply for e-VOA, enjoy Bali, decide before the deadline whether to file the extension
- **Why not visa-free?** Australians are not eligible for it, and even if they were, uncertainty calls for the option of an extension

**Outcome:** Flexibility up to a hard limit of 60 days. Past that, they must leave or hold a different visa.

---

### Scenario 4: Malaysian Remote Worker, 90 Days

**Profile:** Malaysian, remote software engineer, wants 90 days in Canggu

**Best choice:** Neither route

- B1 stops at 60 days, and visa-free stops at 30 days
- Both are tourist routes and are not work authorisations
- A 90-day stay with work needs a different visa, assessed **before** travel

**Not recommended:** chaining visa-free entries or B1 visits with trips abroad. Each entry is decided at the border, and nothing guarantees re-entry.

**Outcome:** Choose the right visa first; do not stretch a tourist route.

### Scenario 5: Thai Family, 20-Day Holiday but Maybe 35 Days

**Profile:** Thai family, booked 20-day Bali trip, but might extend if the kids love it

**Best choice:** B1 / E-VOA

- **Duration:** 30 days, plus the one extension of up to 30 days if approved
- **Process:** Apply for e-VOA for each traveller, enjoy the trip, file the extension on time if you want to stay beyond day 30
- **Why not visa-free?** Thais are eligible, but visa-free stops at 30 days with no extension

**Outcome:** The option of an extension is kept open. If they only stay 20 days, nothing more is needed.

### Scenario 6: Canadian Investor, Frequent 60-Day Trips

**Profile:** Canadian, comes to Bali every 3 months for 60 days to manage property investments

**Best choice:** Assess a visa suited to the purpose, rather than B1 each time

- **Duration:** 60 days is the B1 maximum, reachable only with the one extension and its approval
- **Why not visa-free?** It stops at 30 days
- **Risk:** A pattern of repeated 60-day B1 visits is not a safe plan: B1 is single-entry and each entry is decided at the border

**Long-term optimization:** Consider:

- **C1 tourist visit visa:** a separate visa for tourism and family visits, with its own, longer rules; whether it fits these trips depends on what he does during them
- **KITAS Investor:** if owning a PT PMA, sponsor yourself (1-2 years)

**Outcome:** B1 fits an occasional visit of up to 60 days, not a standing arrangement.

## Conclusion: Choose Wisely, Travel Freely

The choice between visa-free entry and B1 / E-VOA depends on your nationality, trip length and flexibility needs. Here is the final guidance:

### Choose Visa-Free Entry If:

- ✅ Your nationality qualifies (ASEAN + select countries)
- ✅ You are staying **30 days or less** with certainty
- ✅ You have a fixed return flight and a rigid schedule
- ✅ You are comfortable with no extension being possible

### Choose B1 / E-VOA If:

- ✅ Your nationality qualifies (90+ countries including USA, EU, UK, Australia)
- ✅ Your stay may run past 30 days, **up to 60 days at most**
- ✅ You have uncertain plans and want the option of one extension
- ✅ You accept that the extension is a request to immigration, not a guarantee

### Key Takeaways:

1. **Visa-free is a dead end at 30 days.** No extensions, no conversions, no exceptions.
2. **B1 is 30 days, with one extension of up to 30 days, for 60 days at most.** There is no second extension.
3. **Check your nationality eligibility** on imigrasi.go.id before assuming anything.
4. **For stays over 60 days, neither route works.** Assess a different visa before you travel.
5. **Neither route allows work,** including remote work.

### Final Word:

Visa-free entry suits short visits from eligible nationalities. B1 / E-VOA suits an eligible visit that may need one more month. Plan around the limits, not around hoped-for approvals.
