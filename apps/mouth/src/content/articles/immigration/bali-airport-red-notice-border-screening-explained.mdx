## The Facts

A reported Red Notice case at Ngurah Rai Airport illustrates the role of identity checks and information-sharing at the border. It does not announce a new visa rule for ordinary travellers.

## The reported Bali case

IDN Times reported on 24 November 2026 that Ngurah Rai Immigration recorded 44 foreign nationals named in Red Notices attempting to enter Bali between January and 24 November. That is a cumulative figure, not 44 cases on one day.

The report describes a Russian national identified by the initials IG, intercepted after arriving from Phuket on 11 November and deported to Moscow on 18 November. It attributes the account to immigration officials and describes coordination with the Indonesian police's international-relations division and Russian representatives.

The underlying allegations involve fraud and embezzlement. The article does not establish a criminal conviction, and this report does not make one.

## What a Red Notice does—and does not—mean

INTERPOL defines a Red Notice as a request to law-enforcement authorities to locate and provisionally arrest a person pending extradition, surrender or a similar legal action. It is not an international arrest warrant.

INTERPOL also distinguishes a person wanted for prosecution from someone wanted to serve a sentence. A person sought for prosecution has not been convicted and must be presumed innocent until proven guilty. Member countries apply their own laws when deciding what action to take.

Consequently, a Red Notice, an immigration decision and a criminal court judgment are separate matters. A news report should not collapse them into a single finding of guilt.

## Practical relevance for expats and frequent travellers

The ordinary lesson is to keep identity and travel records consistent. Check that names, passport details and travel documentation match. If you know of an unresolved legal matter or an identity error, obtain advice from a qualified professional before travelling.

Do not rely on a visa approval as an answer to every possible border issue. Equally, this particular case is no basis for suggesting that all foreign arrivals face a new ban or that every expat needs a new clearance document.

## Read the scope of the announcement

When assessing similar headlines, ask whether the number refers to arrivals, attempted arrivals, alerts or completed removals; identify the reporting period; and distinguish allegations from judgments.

Those checks prevent a cumulative enforcement statistic from becoming an exaggerated daily threat. For most readers, the useful takeaway is accurate documentation and careful reading of the actual announcement.
