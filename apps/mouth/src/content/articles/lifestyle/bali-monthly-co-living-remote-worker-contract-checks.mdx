## The Facts

Co-living provider Cove reported a 198% rise in monthly bookings from freelancers and self-employed people since the start of 2026, according to Vakansi's coverage of a company statement dated 11 November.

The same report says almost 90% of Cove's self-employed residents chose Greater Jakarta. The figure therefore should not be used as a measure of Bali's rental market, expat arrivals or island-wide co-living growth. It is company-reported data about a particular customer group.

The useful lesson for a remote worker considering Bali is the importance of flexibility. It is not evidence that any particular Bali property is cheaper or better.

---

## In Practice

Compare the total cost of a short stay with the commitment required for a longer contract. Include the deposit, electricity, internet, cleaning, linen, parking and any administrative charges in the comparison.

Ask what “monthly” means in the agreement. Check the notice period, renewal deadline, rate changes and any minimum commitment. A listing that advertises a monthly price may still require advance payment or conditions that affect early departure.

Test the working environment before committing: use a video call from the actual room, check the desk and lighting, and ask about backup arrangements during an internet or power interruption.

## Bali Zero Take

### Our Analysis

Flexibility has value when work and travel plans may change. But the right comparison is between written contracts, not between headline monthly prices.

For example, a slightly lower advertised rent can become less attractive if the deposit is difficult to recover or the contract offers no clear early-exit process. This is a budgeting example, not a statement about Cove's terms or any named Bali operator.
