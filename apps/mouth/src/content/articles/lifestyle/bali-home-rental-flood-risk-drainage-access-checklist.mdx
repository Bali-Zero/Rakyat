## TL;DR

**Before renting a Bali home, check drainage, previous water entry and access. Recent BPBD reporting provides local context, not a parcel-level safety assessment.**

---

## The Facts

ANTARA’s 17 November 2026 report quotes BPBD Bali on recent local flooding and drainage issues. BNPB’s national update on 22 November separately advises people in flood-prone locations to keep drainage clear and monitor official forecasts and warnings. The Bali incident in that later national update concerns a fire in Karangasem; it is not evidence of a new Bali flood on 22 November.

These sources support a practical preparedness discussion. They do not provide a parcel-level flood assessment for a home being offered for rent or sale.

---

## In Practice

When viewing a property, ask the owner about previous water entry: where it occurred, how deep it was, what was repaired and whether there are dated records. Inspect the access lane and drainage route as well as the interior. If possible, return after rain without entering unsafe conditions.

Ask who maintains drains, pumps and shared access, and put repair responsibilities into the tenancy agreement. Check whether alternative access remains usable if the normal route is affected. A beautiful elevated living area does not by itself answer questions about the road, parking or utility equipment below it.

For a higher-value decision, obtain an appropriate site assessment rather than relying on a sales assurance. Keep official weather and local emergency information available after moving in. These are suggested due-diligence steps; they are not a certification of any property’s safety.

---

## What This Does Not Establish

This article is not an evacuation warning, a forecast that every Bali neighbourhood will flood or a claim that a particular property is unsafe. Conditions are local and time-sensitive; follow current official instructions during an incident.
