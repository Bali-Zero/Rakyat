## The Facts

ANTARA reported on 19 November that Bali tourism-office head I Wayan Sumarajaya defended Bali as a destination while saying outside criticism would inform evaluation. The report identifies waste management, traffic and environmental sustainability among the concerns being addressed.

The No List is published by travel media Fodor's. Its recommendations should not be presented as a visa restriction, an airport closure or an official prohibition on visiting Bali. Nor does a positive response from the tourism office guarantee that every visitor will have the same experience.

---

## What a hospitality business can do

Our practical recommendation is to focus on information guests can use. Review whether arrival instructions accurately describe your location and whether your team can explain transport options without promising a fixed journey time.

Review waste arrangements and any environmental claims in your marketing. Keep evidence for statements about your own operation. Specific, supportable information is more useful than simply dismissing criticism or claiming that all concerns have been resolved.

---

## For expats planning visits with family

Treat the debate as a reason to research the particular place and itinerary. Ask accommodation providers about access, transport and local conditions. Separate editorial opinions about a destination from current official travel and immigration information relevant to your own trip.

An island-wide label cannot describe every neighborhood or business. Equally, one attractive photograph does not answer practical questions about getting there or staying there.

---

## Read the two layers separately

The news development is the tourism office's response, reported on 19 November. The wider conversation concerns the quality and sustainability of tourism. Neither should be turned into an unsupported claim about a sudden change to entry requirements.
